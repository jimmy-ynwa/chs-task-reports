Four new names this week, and one of them has never had a store anywhere

Busy week on the tracker. A French Quarter restaurant with a roof that opens, a lingerie label going physical for the first time in nine years, and a café finally escaping the inside of a gym.

New this week

  • Quarter French · French Quarter
    56-seat courtyard under a fully retractable roof — one of the only all-seasons outdoor rooms on Broad.
  • Lorette · Downtown
    Nine years online-only. This is the brand's first physical store anywhere, and they picked Charleston.
  • The Larrikin · Downtown
    Espresso ordered by size — 4, 6 or 10 ounces — instead of cortado, cappuccino, latte.
  • JustFit Kitchen Café · Morrison Yard, Upper Peninsula
    First storefront the public can actually walk into. Café one is locked inside a gym.

Still coming

  • Alteño · Downtown
    The Denver original is Michelin-recommended and made Esquire's 2025 best-new list.
  • Riva Mare · Navy Yard, North Charleston
    Indigo Road goes up on the roof of Storehouse Nine in the Design District.
  • CR Design Center · Mount Pleasant
    45,000 square feet in the box Harris Teeter left — CoStar's Charleston Lease of the Year.

Now open

Nothing flipped open this week. Next Round Social in North Charleston was the last one, and that was two weeks ago.

Two structural gaps

No closings exist. All 26 rows are Type = Opening. The channel promises "opening and closing" and half of it has never appeared. Charleston restaurants close constantly, so this is a sourcing gap, not reality — closings are harder to catch because nobody announces them, which is exactly what makes them worth having. Fix belongs in openings-closings-scan: Eater Carolinas closing coverage, the P&C food section, and ABL licence-transfer notices, which often signal a change of hands before anything is public.
No Rumored rows exist. Zero. Every row is Opening Soon, Announced or Now Open — all publicly known. The field's own description calls Rumored "the members-only tier and the whole reason this table is worth paying for." Right now Friday delivers aggregation, not exclusivity. The SC DOR alcohol beverage licence filings are the fix: public, three to six months ahead of press, nobody local works them systematically. Note the abl-notice-sweep skill already exists and is scheduled Thursdays — its second half files Jimmy's picks as Rumored rows. It just hasn't produced any yet.
