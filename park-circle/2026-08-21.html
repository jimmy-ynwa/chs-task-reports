What this inventory is telling us

Everything on this page is on one level. Nothing else made the page.

Overbrook closed yesterday at $525,000. It asked $559,000, opened at $599,000, and took 79 days to get there. That is the first single-story Park Circle home in this search to actually change hands, and it finally gives the board something the asking prices could not: a real number.

It settled at $281 a foot. The four homes still listed want $329 to $389. Overbrook was the cheapest thing on this page for two straight weeks, and it still gave up 6% off its last ask and 12% off its original before a buyer signed. Everything above it is asking more per foot than the one house that sold.

Three of the four still on the board have already cut — $161,000 between them — and the median has been sitting 75 days. The exception is 1116 Bexley at three days out, and it is the only one that has not yet learned what Overbrook just learned.

The spread is still incoherent. $675,000 buys either 1,825 square feet on Bexley or 2,052 on Princeton with three-tenths of an acre behind it. That is the same money for 227 more feet and nearly double the lot, and Princeton has been sitting on the market for four months while Bexley is brand new. One of those two prices is wrong.

4953 Baylor remains the most interesting property on the page, and it still is not technically in Park Circle. Single-story 3/2 with a detached permitted ADU, blocks off the circle, coded to the generic "North Charleston" subdivision — it surfaces only because of the radius backstop. At $799,000 it is also the only thing here priced against the ceiling rather than the comps.

Nothing here has to happen. But the board now has a closed sale to argue with, and that is more leverage than it had yesterday.
