What this inventory is telling us

Ten homes clear the bar. Every single one has already cut.

That is the number worth sitting with. All 10 listings in Park Circle that meet your size, bed, bath and price criteria have reduced from their original ask — $475,000 in total cuts across the board. Not five of eleven. All of them. In a neighborhood people describe as red hot.

The cuts run from $10,000 to $130,000, and the deepest one belongs to the newest, glossiest house on the list. 4355 Rugheimer opened at $875,000 and sits at $745,000 after 141 days. Park Circle is absorbing what sellers ask for, not what they hope for.

Then the supply problem. Ten homes qualify on paper. Three are actually on one level. Two of the remaining seven are three-story new construction with the kitchen on the second floor, which is the opposite of a downsize. Single-level living in this neighborhood is a genuinely scarce product, and the market has not priced it as scarce yet — the three that qualify are spread from $559,000 to $729,000 with no coherent logic tying them together.

The $/sf spread tells the same story: $256 to $412 inside one small neighborhood. At the top, 4615 O'Hear is selling a historic-district address and a walk to East Montague on the smallest qualifying floor plan. At the bottom, 5332 Parkside is selling a mother-in-law suite and 180 days of patience.

One more thing. The single most interesting property this week is not technically in Park Circle at all — 4953 Baylor Street, a single-story 3/2 with a detached, permitted ADU, sits a few blocks off the circle and is coded to the generic "North Charleston" subdivision. It is the only property found that hits every criterion including the ADU. It surfaced only because of the radius backstop.
