What this inventory is telling us

Everything on this page is on one level. Nothing else made the page.

Ten homes in Park Circle clear your size, bed, bath and price bar. Three of them are on one level. Two of the seven that got cut are three-story new construction with the kitchen on the second floor, which is the opposite of a downsize. Single-level living here is a genuinely scarce product.

The market is softer than the neighborhood's reputation suggests. All ten of those homes have reduced from their original ask — $475,000 in total cuts, running from $10,000 to $130,000. Not five of ten. All of them. The deepest cut belongs to the newest, glossiest house in the group, which opened at $875,000 and now sits at $745,000 after 141 days.

Inside the single-story set the same softness holds: all 4 have cut, $161,000 between them. Nobody here is holding firm.

The pricing has no coherent logic yet. Four homes, $559K to $799K, and $/sq ft running from $300 to $412. At the top, 4615 O'Hear is buying a historic-district address and a walk to East Montague on the smallest floor plan on the page. At the bottom, 4512 Overbrook is the only one under $600,000 — and it is already under contract.

The most interesting property is not technically in Park Circle. 4953 Baylor Street, a single-story 3/2 with a detached permitted ADU, sits a few blocks off the circle and is coded to the generic "North Charleston" subdivision. It hits every criterion including the ADU, and it surfaced only because of the radius backstop.
