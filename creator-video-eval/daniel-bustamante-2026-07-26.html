Quick Read

Daniel Bustamante (about 37K LinkedIn followers, a 12K email list, runs marketing at the Velocity agency) walks through five tactics for turning LinkedIn attention into email subscribers, with Dickie Bush hosting a live cohort session: in-post CTAs, the Featured section, the custom profile button, welcome DMs, and the headline act, the "viral drop." It reached you the way the tactic works, as Daniel's own lead magnet DM'd to you after you commented, and it's the source of the links-in-post question you raised.

Relevance to where Jimmy is now: High

This is a start-to-finish playbook for the exact motion you are building right now, LinkedIn traffic into newsletter subscribers via a gated lead magnet, and several tactics map onto pieces you already have live: the Guide, the ManyChat "Comment GUIDE" trigger, and the Kit welcome sequences.

Key Takeaways

  • ~5:40Links in a post no longer tank reach; the real cost is a small engagement dip, since some readers click out before they like, not a distribution hit. Use in-post links when the job is capture.
    The primary source for your reach-vs-conversion portfolio call. Confirms linking to the Guide in-post is fine when the goal is signups.
  • ~10:10In Featured, use your best lead magnet, not a bare "subscribe" link. Lead magnets out-optin newsletters because the value is immediate and concrete. Keep it to one, minimal copy, custom thumbnail.
    You just added the Guide to Featured. This validates it and says trim to one item and give it a strong image.
  • ~16:04The custom profile button ("Visit my website") puts a persistent CTA on every post and every comment you leave.
    A free, always-on funnel to the Guide gate that you are not using yet.
  • ~19:09Welcome DM to every new connection: thank them, say what you write about, drop 2-3 of your best recent posts. Manual, refreshed every few weeks.
    A low-lift relationship touch that fits your BD instinct and quietly feeds the list.
  • ~25:14The viral drop, the core engine: a hook plus original-image post that teases a valuable asset, asks for a comment, then DMs each commenter the landing-page link. Plain page, 80-90% optin.
    This is the automated version of your "Comment GUIDE" play. The mechanic is the one you are already half-running.
  • ~32:27The DM script closes with "mind letting me know if the link works?" A curiosity loop that measurably lifts optins and pulls extra replies.
    A one-line tweak to your ManyChat auto-DM that raises both conversion and comment velocity.
  • ~34:16Simple landing pages beat fancy ones. The selling already happened in the post, so keep the page congruent (same colors, words, profile pic) and frictionless.
    A direct spec for your Guide gate. Do not overbuild it.
  • ~43:18Seven lead-magnet ideation frameworks: expand, compress, replicate, unbundle, curate, analyze, content series (build the asset in public, e.g. 30 prompts in 30 days).
    A reusable ideation toolkit. "Content series" is operate-as-content, and "expand" is exactly how the Insider's Guide could spawn its next asset.

Viral Drop

A daily-posting operator turned LinkedIn attention into a 12K list by engineering "viral drops," hook posts that trade a comment for a DM'd landing-page link at 80-90% optin.

Creator

Daniel Bustamante, runs marketing at the Velocity agency, about 37K LinkedIn followers and a 12K list built since July 2024. Video hosted by Dickie Bush, who is already in your orbit.

What it is

A repeatable LinkedIn-to-email engine: post a hook plus original image teasing a free asset, ask for a comment, DM each commenter the landing-page link, convert on a deliberately plain page, then bridge into a welcome sequence.

Architecture

Free layer is daily LinkedIn posts for reach and authority. Conversion layer is the drop itself: comment, then DM, then landing page, then optin. Product layer (agency, cohort program, calls) sits behind the list. The loop is the same shape as CHS to TFG, and as your LinkedIn to Guide to CHS-list logic: free high-frequency value builds the audience, a gated asset converts a slice to owned email, revenue waits downstream. The one real difference is that capture here is a discrete engineered event, not a passive footer link.

Monetization

Attention first, list second, dollars last, and he says it outright: likes are good, subscribers are better, dollars are better still. The drops and lead magnets are pure marketing; revenue comes from the agency, the cohort program, and high-ticket calls the list feeds. The list is explicitly the asset; the drop just fills it faster.

Skill-stack

The rare part is hook craft and the discipline to run drops over and over (he has done hundreds). The learnable, packaging part, lead-magnet ideation, simple pages, the email bridge, you largely already own from the CHS stack, and the ManyChat capture is your automated version of his manual comment-to-DM. The BD instinct from 31 years in CRE maps onto his manual reach-outs to good-fit commenters. What you would build is the drop muscle itself: the hook-plus-tease post and the capture loop, which you would automate rather than hand-crank.

Temperament (Welsh ↔ Bush)

Leans Bush. Daily posting, hundreds of drops, a brother handling manual DM labor, a portfolio of lead magnets, 200 outbound connection requests a week. High volume and admittedly grind-y (he calls it a grind himself). Steal the mechanics, not the intensity: your van der Blom-informed 3x-a-week cadence is the opposite pole, and the drop works fine at low volume.

What to steal

  • The viral-drop loop, which you are already half-running with "Comment GUIDE." Add the two-step (link in the post and a link in your own top reply) so you catch both the clickers and the commenters.
  • The DM curiosity-loop line: end the auto-DM with "mind letting me know if the link works?" It lifts optins and pulls extra replies. A free tweak to your ManyChat script.
  • Keep the Guide gate deliberately plain and congruent (same colors, same words, your face), since the post does the selling. Fancy pages convert worse.
  • The seven lead-magnet ideation frameworks as a standing toolkit. "Expand" (turn an overperforming post into a guide) and "content series" (build the next asset in public) are the two most on-thesis for you. Steal-worthy as a whole system to build your own version of, a future Module Inventory candidate once you have run it, and likely a sibling of the 017 Audience-to-Lead-Magnet candidate rather than a new number.
  • The custom profile button and a one-strong-lead-magnet Featured setup. Both are free, always-on funnels to the Guide.

What doesn't map

The daily cadence and hundreds-of-drops volume run against your lean, presence-valuing 3x-a-week model, so do not adopt the grind. The manual DM labor at his scale is a two-person agency's choice; you would automate it. And their money comes from an agency plus a cohort program plus high-ticket calls, a different engine than your newsletter-plus-New-NEXT build, so treat the monetization layer as context, not template.

Why it passes the Anywhere test

Everything, the posting, the drops, the DMs, the landing pages, the email, runs over the internet with no local market or physical presence. Daniel could run the whole motion from a 90-day stint anywhere with a laptop and a phone. The only bottleneck is manual DM time, which automation removes.
