The honest read

This is a first placement doing what a first placement rarely does.

The usual pattern for a sponsored block here is one or two clicks per thousand openers. Hers did 6.9 per thousand. Three things look like they drove it, and they're worth knowing before writing the next two ads.

  • The audience is the buyer. This list skews female, older and affluent — the mother-of-the-bride and the milestone-party host. Event rentals is one of the few categories where the reader is the customer, not a referral to one. TRIO was selling agency retainers to the same list; that's a much longer walk.
  • Top-of-issue billing is worth real money. Logo in the header, block seated ahead of the lead story. That slot has been worth roughly a click per thousand openers more than mid-issue every time it has been measured.
  • The copy asked, it didn't qualify. The reworked "custom" line pointed at table numbers and tumblers — something concrete the reader can picture wanting — rather than asking whether they're a fit.

One caveat to keep in the back pocket: the issue landed Friday rather than Thursday and posted the newsletter's best click rate in three weeks. Some of her lift is that the whole issue clicked well. Her share of clickers strips that out and it still reads 7.1%, so the effect is real, not borrowed.

What her $500 is buying

The three-placement package is $500 net against a $1,000 face rate. Spread evenly, this placement's share is about $167. Against the full 12,527 impressions and 69 clicks:

  • $13.33 CPM across delivered email plus web post views
  • $20.78 per thousand actual email opens — still the most meaningful single number, since 70.8% of the list opened it
  • $2.42 per click through to her site, email and web combined

Two and a half dollars a click for a local business selling four-figure event rentals is the renewal argument, and it doesn't need dressing up. Worth holding until the campaign wrap on October 12 rather than spending it now — one placement is a data point, three is a case.

One thing that went right, quietly

Her link was UTM-tagged: utm_source=chshappenings. TRIO's wasn't, and that cost that campaign its whole after-the-click story.
