Worth a closer look

Old plastic supply piping

The disclosure marks both copper and polybutylene. Polybutylene fails at the fittings, many insurers won't write homeowners coverage on it, and it can come up in lender review. Have a plumber scope it and price a repipe before the inspection window closes.

An encroachment issue from 1960, still open

The seller wrote in that it's "being dealt with." Ask for the current survey and exactly what's being done, by whom, and when.

The seller declined to answer on flooding

Both flood questions on the disclosure came back "no representation," along with code violations and beach assessments. The seller did confirm carrying flood insurance and never filing a claim. And the federal flood policy caps building coverage at $250,000, so on a house this price excess flood coverage would be needed.

The listing and the disclosure disagree on the HOA

The listing flags an association. The disclosure says there isn't one. Clear it up before relying on either.

Checks out

Solid

On public sewer, the only one of the three. Roof half replaced in 2025 with the rest repaired in 2026. Recent floors, refinished hardwood and new appliances upstairs. No known water, plumbing, electrical or structural problems reported, and no flood claims or disaster assistance.

Good to know

Two sets of rental numbers

The listing quotes the owner's past rental revenue at about twice what the management company's own projection shows, and that projection was run against a higher price than today's ask, with no owner use. Its own cap-rate estimate is 2%. About half the year's revenue lands in June, July and August. No bookings transfer with the house.
