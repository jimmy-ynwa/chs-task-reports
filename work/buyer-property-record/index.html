01The short version

Four facts, before anything else

  • The living floor sits one-tenth of a foot above the flood line. That's the whole cushion.
  • The LLC selling it didn't buy it. One person did, in 2012, then moved it into an LLC he controls for $5.
  • Four listings, zero sales, $600,000 off the high point, and still sitting.
  • Zero flood claims on record, back through Hurricane Hugo. A real point in the home's favor.
02The flood record

One number matters more than the zone

Every home on a barrier island sits in or near a flood zone. That's physics, not a red flag. The real question is how high the house sits above the water.

ZoneAE
Flood line (BFE)10 ft
Living floor10.1 ft
New-build code14 ft
What "BFE 10 feet" actually means

It's not a distance off the ground. It's an elevation on a fixed national scale, like sea level: how high FEMA expects water to reach in a flood with a 1-in-100 chance in any year. The ground here sits around 8 feet. The floor sits at 10.1. It clears the older rule it was built under, barely, and nowhere near today's code for new homes.

Hugo hit this island directly. This house has never had a flood claim.

Two honest caveats: the house predates flood insurance, so nothing before 1968 could show up either way, and the policy on file had lapsed and needed a fresh pull. The island's strong FEMA rating already knocks up to 25% off premiums. That's the town earning the discount, not the house.

Two things to confirm

The policy listed an LLC-owned rental as a primary residence, which was likely wrong and could mean a higher premium once corrected. And the elevation certificate showed a small second crawlspace with no flood vents. Ask the inspector what's back there.

03The ownership record

One man, one LLC, one $5 deed

Don't picture an investment group or a builder. The deed and mortgage record tell a simpler story.

2012One person buys the house.
2012–2021Five refinances in his own name, the last two seven months apart.
2022He moves the house into an LLC for $5. He formed the LLC and is its registered agent, at this same address.
2023The LLC takes out a fresh loan seven months after the transfer.

A $5 deed isn't a sale. It's common for short-term rentals and not a red flag on its own. What it means for you: whatever the paperwork says, you're negotiating with one person. We're not going to guess why he refinanced five times. What the public record does show is an active, leveraged property, not a paid-off vacation home someone is reluctantly letting go.

05Before an offer

What we'd run down first

Worth flagging

The county tax record says 3 bed / 2 bath. The listing advertises 5 bed / 4 bath. That gap usually means additions that may or may not be permitted.

  1. Your own flood quoteBefore you're inside a due-diligence clock, not the seller's number.
  2. Ask in writing if it has ever taken waterThe LLC seller skipped the standard disclosure, so a written question is the only way to get anything on record.
  3. Confirm bed and bath count against permitsBefore it's your problem.
  4. Price the tax changeBought as a second home or rental, the assessment rate jumps from 4% to 6%. Get a real number from the assessor.
