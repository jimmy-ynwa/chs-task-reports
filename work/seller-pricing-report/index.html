01What changed

Three weeks is usually too short to matter. This time it mattered

Five of the six listings competing with this home either cut their price or went under contract. A newer, larger three-bedroom came on above all of them.

1New competitor, at $440,000

The listing we watched most closely was asking $420,000 in July. If it had sold near that, it would have supported the top of our range. Instead it cut to $389,900, down 9.3% over 91 days, and it still hasn't found a buyer.

Why one listing sets the ceiling

The closest match in the whole community is a three-bedroom end unit about 80 square feet larger. It's been on the market 199 days, is down 10.1%, and is now offering to buy down a buyer's rate. Any price at or above $385,000 asks a buyer to pick this home over one the market has already passed on at that number. That's the single strongest limit on where we start.

03Timing

When homes here sell, they sell fast. When they don't, they sit

We compared the community's nine closed sales with every comparable sale on the island in the same size and price lane: 1,400 to 2,100 square feet, $325,000 to $475,000.

That isn't a slow-neighborhood problem. It's a pricing problem.

The pattern here is consistent: list high, sit two or three months, cut nine or ten percent, then sell. The sellers who priced right on day one skipped all of it. One listed at $365,000 and went under contract at full ask in 33 days. Another trimmed once and was under contract in 21.

The biggest open variable is condition

Two homes here are nearly identical on paper. One sold for $345,000 in poor condition. The other, within four square feet, sold for $449,500 fully updated. That's a $104,500 spread on condition alone. Until we walk the home once the tenants are out, the range assumes it still shows close to how it was bought, less a few years of rental wear.

05Pricing strategy

Where we recommend starting

The cost of starting too high isn't a lower price later. It's a lower price later, plus three months of days on market every buyer can see.

Recommended

List at $379,900

The clear value in the community instead of one more listing in the queue. Expect a contract in 30 to 45 days, trading between $370,000 and $378,000.

Alternative

$389,900, with a cut already scheduled

Test the top of the range, but commit up front to $374,900 at day 21 rather than letting days pile up.

What we would not recommend

$399,000 or above. Every listing that tried the $400,000s here in the past year ended up cutting nine or ten percent to find a buyer, and two are still looking.

Next steps

  1. Walk the home once the tenants are outConfirm where condition puts it in the range.
  2. Agree on price and pre-market workPaint and flooring are the two highest-return items in this community.
  3. PaperworkListing agreement in place.
  4. Set the list date around the owners' travelListing before a mid-September trip catches about six more weeks of fall buyers. A late-September launch puts closing in December, the slowest stretch of the year.
