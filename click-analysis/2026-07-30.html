Attribution caveat

GA "direct" is a mixed bucket. It holds typed URLs, bookmarks, privacy browsers, ad clicks that lost their tags, and email clicks whose referrer was stripped by the mail client. There is no way from this data to say what share of those 883 sessions came from Thursday's email, and this report does not try to.

What the data does support: email is under-credited here. The lead story alone logged 334 unique email clickers in Beehiiv. GA4's explicitly tagged "newsletter / email" channel logged 30 sessions across the entire property for the whole week. Those two numbers cannot both be describing the same behavior, so the explicit email tag is capturing a small fraction of real email traffic.

Practical rule: trust Beehiiv for email engagement, trust GA4 for non-email demand, and never add the two together. External links — venues, Eventbrite, City Paper, marketchs.com — never touch this GA4 property at all, so their only measurement is the Beehiiv click count above.

Takeaways

  • The lead was right, and it was a photo finish Route 41 pulled 391 clicks. Charleston Place pulled 396. A five-click gap on 1,914 total is a tie, not a lead-selection miss — the Verdict hook did its job and CTOR landed at 9.26%, a full point above the 8.3% baseline. Both stories were Tier 1 material; either could have led.
  • ICYMI is doing more work than the lead, again The five ICYMI items pulled 848 clicks, 44.3% of everything, versus 20.4% for the lead. That is the structural pattern, not a one-week fluke. The ICYMI slot is the highest-leverage real estate in the issue and should be treated as such when picking stories.
  • Events drew 10.2% and it is broad and shallow 195 clicks spread across 12 venue links, top one at 32. Nobody link is a destination — readers are browsing the block, not chasing a single event. That is fine for utility but it means no individual venue link is worth pitching as a sponsor placement on click volume alone.
  • Route 41 and Charleston Place both over-indexed on non-email Both landed roughly 600 GA4 users against roughly 340 unique email clickers — 1.7 to 1.8x more web demand than email demand. Nextdoor sent 692 sessions and 641 users that week, second only to direct. These are the push-on-paid candidates: civic conflict and luxury-development stories travel outside the list.
  • The happy hour ask converted at 91 clicks Fifth-highest link in the issue, ahead of two ICYMI stories and every event. When the ask is specific and dated, readers act. Worth remembering for the community play launching next week.
  • Media kit got 19 clicks and marketchs got 34 Small but real. Marketchs at 34 out-clicked every event link and every venue in the issue, which is a decent argument for keeping the TFG real-estate blurb where it sits.
