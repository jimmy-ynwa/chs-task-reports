The issue went to 11,146 Charleston-area inboxes and 8,063 people opened it. That's a 72.3% open rate. The industry norm for a list like this is 30 to 40%.

MELT got two spots in the email. I gave it a shout-out in my intro at the very top of the issue, and it had the Featured Event block right after the weekend events list. Both linked to your festival homepage, meltmuralfestival.com. The Featured Event card also ran near the bottom of all five stories on chshappenings.com that week and on the issue's own web page. That added another 1,115 page views, for 9,178 total impressions.

90 clicks went to your homepage: 38 from my intro, 38 from the Featured Event block and 14 from the website. Each link's count is unique people. Someone who tapped both email links is counted once for each.

The honest read

This one landed. A sponsored spot here usually gets one or two clicks for every thousand people who open the issue. MELT got about 9, counting both email links, and the Featured Event block on its own got nearly 5. That's the strongest reader response to a sponsored spot I've measured.

Why I think it worked. It was free, it had a date two days out, and it gave people something to picture: live painting all weekend and a block party at the end. The intro mention also put MELT in front of readers before they'd scrolled at all.

A bonus I kept out of the totals. MELT also sat in my regular weekend events list, linked to the Eventbrite page. 25 more people clicked there. That was part of my usual listings, not your feature, so it isn't in the 90.

What did less. On the website, the card sat at the bottom of the stories, and two of the five stories sent no clicks. The email did the heavy lifting, which is normal for an event.

What I'd do next time

Run it twice. An early save-the-date a couple of weeks out, then a “this weekend” reminder. The first gets it on calendars; the second catches everyone who meant to go.

Lead with a mural. A photo of the finished walls would give the block the same pull the words did.
