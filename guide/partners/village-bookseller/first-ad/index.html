Three changes I made to your idea, and why

These aren’t options. All three are already built into the ad below, and each one is based on how buyers actually think.

1

Show the reading, not just “staff picks”

How buyers think: people trust what took real effort and tune out what anyone could say. Every store and every list says “staff picks” this time of year. What a website can’t copy is a person who read all year and chose one book.

“[Name] read [N] books this year. This is the one.”

2

Say who each book is for

How buyers think: in November, your readers are shopping for other people. They aren’t looking for the best book of the year. They’re looking for a gift that won’t miss. A pick that names the person it’s for takes the guesswork out.

“For the dad who only reads history.” “For the friend who swears she has no time to read.”

3

A photo of handwriting instead of headshots

How buyers think: handwriting reads as care, because it obviously took a person’s time. Books standing on your counter, each with a handwritten card from the bookseller who chose it, will out-sell smiling faces. It’s also one phone photo, not a photo shoot. Keep the cards by the books afterward, so readers who come in find exactly what the ad showed them.

Couldn’t put it down. Read it twice– [Name]
For the dad who only reads history– [Name]
My book of the year, no contest– [Name]
