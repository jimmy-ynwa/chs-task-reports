The Kids On Point placement ran in six places that week: the Thursday email itself, plus the lead story and four of the five "in case you missed it" pieces on chshappenings.com. (The fifth ICYMI story that week — the Live5News piece on the new Johns Island hotel — linked straight to Live5News's own site rather than a CHS page, so it never carried the card.) Every CHS-hosted page carried your card — so readers saw it whether they opened the email, clicked into a story from the site, or found one through search or social.

Altogether that's just over 11,000 subscribers reached by email (70.9% open rate — well above the 30–40% typical for this kind of list) plus another 930 page views across the six web pages, for 8,906 total impressions and 20 clicks back to Kids On Point or the Buck For Kids Day page.

Given the timing — this ran the day before the event, with a single simple ask ("order your regular coffee, $1 goes to the kids") — that's a strong response for a placement with one day to do its job.

Why it landed

No donation form, no extra step — just a normal coffee order with the giving built in. That kind of low-friction ask tends to convert better than anything that asks a reader to stop and do something new, and the numbers here bear that out.

Thanks for bringing this to me

Kids On Point does great work for Lowcountry kids, and I'm glad I could help get the word out for Buck For Kids Day. Happy to talk through what a longer-term partnership could look like whenever it's useful.
