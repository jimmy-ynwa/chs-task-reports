Both issues beat our own recent average. CHS Happenings has been running a 66.7% open rate over the past four weeks. TRIO's two issues came in at 70.5% and 71.3% — the strong half of the summer, not the quiet half. For reference, the newsletter industry norm is 30–40%.

Clicks are unique people, not repeat taps — August was 10 clicks from the email plus 1 from the web edition. Share of clickers is TRIO's slice of everyone who clicked anything at all in that issue. It's the fairest way to judge an ad, because it takes out how busy the issue itself happened to be.

The honest read

Clicks went from 11 to 7, and that looks worse than it was.

The September 3 issue clicked less across the board. Every link in it — stories, events, everything — pulled about 24% fewer clicks than the August 20 issue did. Against that backdrop TRIO essentially held its ground: 1.22% of all clickers in August, 1.12% in September. The ad didn't wear out. It got a smaller pool to work with.

What actually moved was position. On August 20, TRIO had the full top-of-issue treatment — the logo sitting in the header above any story copy, and the sponsor block placed immediately before the lead feature. On September 3 it ran in the mid-issue slot, after the lead story and before the roundup. That difference is consistent and measurable: 1.24 clicks per thousand readers who opened, versus 0.86.

The calls to action read differently too, and it's worth naming. August asked people to “See what they'd do for your brand.” September asked them to “Find out if you're a fit.” Same audience, same length, same claims — but the second one quietly asks the reader to decide whether they qualify before they click. With this audience, the invitation outpulled the filter.

Two things I'd change next time

Fix this firstTag the link

Both ads pointed at trio-solutions.com with no tracking tag on the link. Our platform counted the clicks, so the numbers above are solid — but on your end those visitors are invisible. You can't see them land, can't follow them to a contact form, and can't put a dollar figure on the traffic. A tagged link costs nothing to add and turns “18 clicks” into something you can actually evaluate against everything else you're running.

Worth knowingThe list dipped slightly during the run

Delivered volume went from 11,477 to 11,416 between the two sends. It's a small move and it didn't affect your open rates — September actually opened better than August — but you should hear it from me rather than not hear it at all.

If there's a next run

You'd start it knowing two things neither of us knew in August: the Primary slot is where the money goes, and an invitational call to action outpulls a qualifying one with these readers. That's a real head start over guessing, and it's the argument for treating a second campaign as a continuation rather than a fresh test.

The other thing frequency buys is recognition. Two placements introduce a name. It takes more than two before readers stop reading “TRIO” as an ad and start reading it as a Charleston business they've seen around.
