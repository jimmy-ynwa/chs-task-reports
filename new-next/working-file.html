The van der Blom playbook

The tone and tactics you're adopting, pulled from his own stated principles. The North Star already absorbed the strategic spine of this. What follows is the execution detail underneath it.

Tone
Research before opinion. Patterns over posts. Calm over noise. Application without hype. No urgency tactics, no "LinkedIn just changed everything" headlines. Optimise for signal, not trends. His Triple A: Authentic, Active, Approachable
The anti-pitch
Sell implicitly. One or two CTAs max. Earn the attention first. His line: nobody owes you their attention. The sell rides inside the value, it is never the value
Depth beats slop
Generic AI content gets deprioritized. The platform is rebuilt to reward substance, which is the whole reason your teardowns win here
T1Optimise for the right signals
Dwell time, saves, and conversations. Not likes, and not flat comments (those now cost you −9%). Saves lead every other signal and the lead just widened, with under 3% of posts earning even one. A reply that sparks 2+ more replies is what the algorithm counts
T2The first 60 to 90 minutes decide it
Early engagement is the ranking trigger. Post when you can be present to reply, and reply fast. A quiet first hour caps the post's ceiling
T3Personal profile beats company page, badly
His read: companies leave 80% on the table by posting from the page. Reach lives on the human account. This is the data behind posting as Jimmy, not as the entity
T4Links cost reach, and the comment workaround is dead
Sharper Aug 2026 numbers supersede the earlier read. One external link in the post body cuts median reach 18.8% (1.3M posts). And the old "put it in the first comment" dodge no longer works: LinkedIn now suppresses comments containing external links by up to 80%. Countervailing signal worth holding: a Q1 2026 SayWhat study of 400K posts found posts with multiple external links beat posts with none, so this isn't a clean "never link" rule. Treat a link as a deliberate reach cost you pay when the job is capture

Carousels, now resolved (Oct 2026): he's unambiguous — carousels are down over 40% since the start of 2026, and anyone still calling them the top format is "copy-pasting the 2025 playbook." That settles the earlier tension; the 6.60% document-post figure was the stale reading. Carousels are off the New NEXT feed. The format now being boosted is "Celebrate an Occasion," 25-45% better than plain text plus image, so aggressively that 1,200+ posts used it with nothing to celebrate. Working rule: use it when there's a real occasion (a launch, a milestone, shipping something) and never when there isn't. Faking an occasion is exactly the performative tactic his own research says gets weeded out, and it would break the calm register you're adopting for a short-lived exploit

2026 context in his research: LinkedIn rebuilt around an interest graph with LLM ranking, roughly March 2026. Organic views down about 50%, engagement about 25%, follower growth about 59% year over year. The takeaway is not despair, it is that reach is now earned by depth and dwell, which favors your teardown-and-lesson approach over volume

His July 2026 update — what's scaling vs tanking

Scaling: personal takes with original images +30% (AI can't fake them). Saves lead every signal, under 3% of posts earn one. Reach no longer capped by network size, it travels on topic match, so a small account can out-distribute a big one. Comments that spark 2+ replies +25%. Links to in-depth content +21%.

Tanking: flat comments −9%. Topic drift, on-topic 8/10 beats 6/10 by 30%. Carousels −40% reach, −30% engagement. Plain reshares −20 to −30%. Off-topic comments actively suppress a post.

Net for you: attach a real screenshot to every teardown (+30%), stay ruthlessly in lane (the firewall is now a ranking advantage, not just discipline), turn first-hour comments into threads, link to depth, and never run a carousel here.

Links, the reconciliation (vdB vs Bustamante): both are 2026 data-informed and they still disagree, so resolve it by purpose, not by rule. Reach-optimised posts carry no link and maximise saves. Conversion-optimised posts carry the link and accept the reach hit, because reach you can't convert is vanity and the list is the business. Start near a 2:1 reach-to-conversion split across the week, then let your own signup-vs-save data set it. What's dead either way: the lazy middle, dropping a link in the first comment and assuming it's free.

October 2026 report — the six trends

1. AI comments punished hard. A comment flagged as AI loses 88% of its reach impact, and your next genuine comment starts with a 15% penalty. LinkedIn is building a trust score around commenting behaviour. Never let anything but you write a comment.

2. The Interest Graph sorts by topic, and format decides how far outside your network you travel. On-topic niche posts reach 65% outside your network. Personal storytelling reaches only 30% outside, 70% inside. Popular general topics go 80% outside.

3. In-app writing beats pasting. Composing directly in LinkedIn is worth +22% reach vs pasting from an AI tool; even pasting from Word costs 9%. Correlational, no stated mechanism, heavy confound with content quality — but compliance is cheap.

4. Signals compound faster. Watch one video from a creator and you get 20-30% more of their video plus 2 extra posts in your next 25. Video is a wedge that increases later delivery of your ordinary posts to the same person.

5. Video climbing again. 100+ video creators saw 15-25% more reach month over month since March. Keep them under 90 seconds.

6. Carousels down 40%+; "Celebrate an Occasion" boosted 25-45%. See the note above on using it honestly.

The one that changes plans: finding 2. Insight and confession posts are personal storytelling, so they stay 70% inside your existing network. That network is a legacy asset from a former career, not the New NEXT audience. Fix: anchor every insight post to the topic — the confession has to be about the machine, not just about you. Then it reads as on-topic and travels.

Authority vs Insight

Authority builds status. Insight creates recognition. The post that makes a reader see themselves before they finish reading is the one that converts.

From his Newsletter #70, and it's the sharpest lens available for what you're about to write. He ran the A/B on himself, two weeks apart, same audience. A confession post, admitting he'd deleted half his own profile because it was quietly working against him, pulled 24K impressions and comments where readers described their own version of the same mistake. An authority post stacked with Wharton, Gartner, and Forrester citations pulled 11K and comments that were peer compliments.

His read: authority attracts peers and freebie hunters. Insight makes a reader picture their own situation mid-sentence.

The test
Three questions to find an insight post. 1. What did this cost you or a client before you figured it out? A number helps. 2. What were you doing before you knew better — the actual mistake, not the polished version? 3. Would you be comfortable if someone asked a follow-up in the comments?
The signal
If the answer to #3 is no, that's the post. Discomfort is what separates a real confession from a tidy lesson dressed as vulnerability. And it has to be currently uncomfortable, not "here's what I learned twenty years ago"
Your default gravity
This is the one aimed straight at you. Thirty-one years in commercial real estate makes authority the path of least resistance — every draft will pull toward "five lessons from three decades." That's exactly the mode his data says underperforms. Writing insight will take active discipline, not inclination
Untouched material
You and Kim have named residential real estate as work you don't enjoy. Most brokers would never say that out loud. The version that would genuinely cost you something to defend in the comments is where the real insight lives
The caveat
He never ties "converts" to a downstream business outcome. No leads, no calls booked, no purchases — he measures reach and comment quality and takes the rest on faith. Probably earned in his case, but the receipts aren't in the piece. So track both: his leading indicators, and a real downstream action of your own
Sharpens 011
Operate-as-content says the operating is the content. His addition: the operating is only content when it includes what you got wrong. Confession beats credential

The six-element post structure

Same ideas, same frequency. One thing changed: the order.

From his Aug 2026 carousel. He claims 1.8x reach and 2.5x conversion from reordering alone, having gone back through his own posts and found that nearly every high performer carried the same six elements in the same sequence, and the weak ones were missing one or had them out of order.

The key reconciliation: this is not in tension with Authority ÷ Insight. The six elements are the skeleton; Authority vs Insight is the entry point. The structure tells you the order, Newsletter #70 tells you what kind of story goes in slot 4. Tellingly, he labels this an "Authority post" structure but demonstrates it on a confession — losing a €40,000 client to one line in a proposal — which is an insight post by his own framework.

1 · Hook
Nothing below it matters if this fails. Three seconds before a thumb moves on. Make it personal; if you can't, go clearly negative or positive. His: "I lost a €40,000 client because of one line in my proposal"
2 · Promise
A "see more" click isn't won attention, it's the benefit of the doubt. The Promise says what they get for staying. If you can't say it in one line, the post doesn't have one yet. His: "Here's the exact line, and what I'd write instead"
3 · Positioning
The sharpest idea in the post. One line on why anyone should believe you, and it is not your bio. Not "+10 years of experience" but "I've sat in on 50 sales calls this year and this comes up every time." Proximity and recency beat credentials. Rotate the angle (authority, proof, scale, proximity) so it never reads as a slogan. If everybody could have written it, it builds no trust
4 · Core Message
Roughly 70% of the post. Where the value actually lands. Take the time to make the point with a punch, lead with authority, be directive. Don't go too short, don't use jargon, don't assume they know what you mean
5 · Conclusion
Land the point without repeating it. Never make the reader draw their own conclusion. If your last line summarises what you just said, cut it. His: "Clients don't buy scope. They buy a sequence they can picture themselves inside"
6 · Call to Engage
A question, not a pitch. This decides whether the post ends in a scroll or a conversation. The design principle worth stealing: ask a question whose answer reveals a problem the reader has. His: "Are you closing more than 50% of your proposals right now?" — self-diagnostic, mildly uncomfortable, invites confession rather than compliment

Why Positioning matters most for you: it solves the default-gravity problem at the sentence level. You need credibility in a post without the post becoming an authority post. Positioning is exactly that — one line, then move on. So the fix isn't "31 years in commercial real estate," it's the proximity version: "I've shipped this newsletter every Thursday for two years to 11,000 people." Present tense, proof by doing, no credential flex. Same trust, none of the freebie-hunter gravity.

Read it skeptically, though. The 1.8x and 2.5x carry no methodology: no n, no time period, no control. He looked at his own winners and found a shared structure, which is survivorship — good posts may carry these elements naturally rather than being caused by them. "Conversion" is once again undefined, now a pattern in his work rather than a one-off. And the post is a sales asset, ending in a waitlist pitch for a €149 playbook, so the numbers are motivated. He also breaks his own structure to do it, inserting a pitch block between Conclusion and CTE. None of that makes the structure wrong. It does mean treat the six elements as a craft checklist, not a proven multiplier.

Two side observations. He published this as a carousel, the format he told everyone in October was down 40%+, which supports the working read that a carousel is a depth play he'll still pay reach for when the content demands it. And his own showcased Promise line contains a typo ("will land your more deals"). The most-cited LinkedIn authority on the platform shipped it anyway. Take the permission.

The comment ladder

96% of LinkedIn comments do nothing — not because they're bad, but because they never leave level 3.

From his Aug 27 carousel, built on audits of his own account plus 200+ client projects. Six levels, and the impressions climb steeply with each one.

1 · Reaction
A like. Costs nothing, signals nothing. Impact close to zero, and it shouldn't count toward any daily target
2 · Reply
"Great post." "So true." Registers as presence, not expertise. Zero profile visits, zero follows. His example: 157 impressions
3 · Real comment
A genuine point, a data point, a real experience or question. This is the minimum bar; below it nothing counts. Still low authority. 6,674 impressions
4 · Resonance
Specific enough that the author replies directly. This is where profile visits and follows start happening on their own. 8,698 impressions
5 · Conversation
A real back-and-forth with the author, in the thread or moved to DM. High authority. 12,408 impressions
6 · Discussion
Your comment stops needing the author. Other readers reply to you directly, building a thread of its own inside someone else's post. High impression, high authority. 19,859 impressions

Three rules for writing level 5 and 6: start with something true for you (if the topic doesn't connect to something you've lived, the first line reads like every other comment); add something the post didn't already say (if anyone who read it could have written your comment, it adds nothing); and close with a statement, not a question — every AI comment now ends in a question and readers can smell it. Say what you think.

Don't let that collide with the CTE rule. Different surfaces. On your own post, end with a question (Call to Engage). On someone else's post, end with a statement. The reason for the second one is that the tell has flipped: questions used to read as engaged, now they read as automated.

The confound he doesn't address. His level examples are single cherry-picked comments, not averages, and comment impressions scale with the host post's reach. The level 6 example sits on a Gary Vaynerchuk post carrying 639 comments and 1,213 reactions — much of that 19,859 is Vaynerchuk's distribution, not comment craft. So the ladder is real but incomplete: post selection is at least as big a lever as comment quality. A level 6 comment on a 200-impression post earns nothing. His own "right people, right timing" advice quietly concedes this.

Usual caveats. The 96% has no definition of "do nothing" — no profile visit, no follow, no reply, unstated. "Time dropped 80%, conversations doubled" is self-reported with no baseline, and "conversations" is undefined, same as "converts" has been throughout. The post is again a sales asset, this time with early-bird pricing and a live link. Worth noting he put that external link in the post body, paying the 18.8% reach cost his own research documents — a live demonstration of the reach-versus-conversion trade already in this file.

The Bustamante mechanics

From CE-007. Van der Blom supplies the cadence, the register, and the algorithm read; Bustamante supplies the capture plumbing underneath it. Steal the mechanics, not the intensity — his growth is 24 months of daily posting and 200 outbound connection requests a week, which is the opposite pole from where you sit.

Hook formula
Moment in time + credibility + big outcome + secret. "Last week [moment] we helped a client [credibility] drive [outcome] using a tactic I discovered back [secret]." The most portable template in the session, and it dovetails with van der Blom's Positioning element rather than fighting it — the credibility slot is recent and specific, which is proximity, not a credential flex
The viral drop
A hook-plus-image post teasing a free asset, asking for a comment, then DMing each commenter the landing-page link. Plain page, high optin because the post already did the selling. This is your Instagram "Comment CHS" play, ported
First drop
A book curation. Universal, works in every niche, requires zero new IP. Run the mechanic once end to end before building a real asset, so you learn what's actually hard about the pipe
Small lifts
Close the delivery DM with "mind letting me know if the link works?" — a curiosity loop that lifts optins and pulls replies. Add a PS to the delivery email inviting a reply. Bump non-responders by hand at 48 hours. All one-line edits with real return
Always-on surfaces
One strong lead magnet in Featured, treated as a thumbnail, not a bare "subscribe" link. Plus the custom profile button, which rides on every post and every comment you leave
Content series
Commit to a 15- or 30-post series, publish one at a time, then bundle the set as a drop. Audience on the way, IP for free, a lead magnet at the end. The LinkedIn analog of Module 011

Three things in CE-007 have gone stale — reconcile, don't paste.

1. The two-step link is half dead. The eval says put the link in the post and in your own top reply. As of van der Blom's October data, comments containing external links are suppressed up to 80%, so the second half now costs more than it returns. Keep the in-post link on conversion posts; drop the comment link.

2. Don't automate the LinkedIn drop. ManyChat handles Instagram, but LinkedIn is now actively hostile to automation — his October finding is that it moves you toward a ban, not just a restriction. Bustamante runs his DMs manually with his brother's help. Yours stay manual too, which caps drop volume and happens to suit the lean model.

3. Verify the profile button still exists. It's a Premium feature, and in the session itself they went looking for it on several profiles and couldn't find it — "maybe they've removed it." Check before budgeting for Premium on that basis alone.

Hook types, ranked

The most-recommended opener on LinkedIn is the second-worst performer measured.

Pierre Bruneau, 6,316 posts, via Favikon; surfaced in van der Blom's Post of the Week. Multipliers are against baseline. This fills a real gap — the six-element structure names the Hook as element 1 but never said which form of opener to reach for.

1.83x · Statistic
The winner, and used in only 10% of top posts. Not "a lot of companies" but "67% of companies." The number is the hook. Scarcity is doing real work here: the best-performing form is the least-used one
1.72x · Product news
A launch, a raise, a release. Works, but only when you genuinely have news
1.21x · Story
"I took my last drink on May 29th, 2010." Reliable, and the second most-used form
1.19x · Direct address
"Your highest performers rarely announce they're leaving." Small sample, but consistently above average
1.16x · Contrarian
"Toxic culture is rarely obvious." Fastest-growing hook on the platform, 5.6% → 8.1% in twelve months — which is also the warning. The scarcity premium that makes the statistic hook win is actively eroding here as everyone piles in
0.88x · Question
Below average, and the single most-advised opener on LinkedIn. If you're leading with a question because a coach told you it drives engagement, the data disagrees
0.87x · Plain observation
Dead last, and used in 59.5% of all posts. This is the default everyone falls into without choosing it

The apparent contradiction, resolved. Question hooks lose (0.88x) but the six-element structure ends every post with a question (the Call to Engage). Both hold, because they're different positions. A question at the top asks the reader to do work before you've given them anything — no reason to answer, easy to scroll. A question at the bottom lands on someone who has already read and now has something to say. So: never open with a question, always close with one. And on other people's posts, close comments with a statement instead.

The convergence worth acting on. Personal career news scored 5.26x — higher than everything — and Bruneau cut it from the ranking honestly, since congratulations aren't a copywriting technique and you can't manufacture a new job weekly. But pair that with van der Blom's October finding that "Celebrate an Occasion" posts run 25-45% better than plain text plus image, and two independent datasets are saying the same thing: LinkedIn structurally boosts genuine announcements. So bank your real milestones instead of letting them pass unremarked — the first 100 on the New NEXT list, the guide relaunch, shipping something. Use the format when it's true, never when it isn't.

Caveats. Van der Blom discloses he's a shareholder in Favikon, the data source — disclosed, to his credit, but he's promoting research from a company he holds equity in. The multiplier isn't defined (impressions? engagement?), and there's no indication it controls for account size, follower count, or topic. The same confound as everywhere else applies: accounts that open with real statistics probably do real research, so the hook may be a marker of quality rather than a cause of it. Bruneau also notes hooks vary a lot by industry, and flags Direct Address as a small sample. He says his own November report will put this under the microscope against his dataset — worth re-checking then.
