03

Selling the unsellable

Nobody buys "relationship building." It's unsellable as a noun, and everyone claims it. You don't sell the soft skill — you sell the system the soft skill got encoded into.

The BD instinct isn't the product. It's the thing that let you build a better product than a technical person would have. The sponsor pre-call sequence, the favor-to-sponsor pivot, the "no personal email until they click or reply" gate — the relationship wisdom is load-bearing in the design decisions. Teach the system; the judgment rides inside it. Show the how, and the why quietly proves the career.

The five pillars

40–50%
Authority · how I built it
Your own machines, opened up: the newsletter that builds itself, the sponsor machine, event ops, the Meta ads engine. A framework, a how-it-works or a case study, built for the save. Can run as a 6 to 8 page document or five or more real screenshots. This is the old "system teardowns" pillar. Gets you found.
15–25%
Story · the expensive lessons
What happened to you, then what it taught. Money lit on fire, the deal you lost, the 33 full names. The career lives here most naturally. Story in, framework out. The proof that you've done the work.
15–20%
Insight · the counter-take
Name a pitfall your buyer hasn't put into words, and argue it fairly with your own numbers. Aim it at your field: newsletters, ads, follow-up, the tools. Never at LinkedIn itself.
~5%
Community · other people's voices
About one Thursday a month. A sponsor's or client's own sentence, a shout-out, a tag of someone who'll comment. Kim's Bloom50 post is the model. Not the co-author feature.
5–10%
Conversion · a clear next step
One or two a month, every three to four weeks. A story that ends in an offer, with the link in the body: Click to Close, jimmyfarrell.com, later the paid set. These reach 6 to 8 times fewer people, so they stay rare. Never a launch post.

The floor: at least one practical post every week, whatever the mix drifts to. Stories bring reach; practical posts get saved 1.6 to 1.9 times more and sent more often. The way in: every pillar opens from a career or personal beat when one fits. Register stays: Know, Trust and Like still label what job a post does.

05

The weekly rhythm

Three posts planned a week; two is the floor. Richard's number-one priority (Nov 2026, §12.1): two or three posts a week collect 2.55x the impressions of a one-post week, at only 6% less per post. You show up in most weeks already; the gap is posts per week. A two-post week is a win, not a miss. Never daily, and leave at least 12 hours between posts.

Days: Tuesday to Thursday for now. Those are average days (1.00x, 0.99x, 0.98x). Sunday reaches 1.14x and Monday 1.05x, and your Nov 16 Monday post did well. Once three-a-week has held four straight weeks, move the story post to Sunday or Monday. Hour: one fixed slot at noon ET, typed and scheduled in LinkedIn itself. No hour beats another for the same person, but a fixed one trains your readers. From February, one post a week goes out at a different hour to reach new people.

06

The only scoreboard

Judge every post on two things

Would someone bookmark this?  ·  Did it move a stranger toward a surface I own?
Not followers. Not likes. Not someone else's viral post: you measure against your own median (676 impressions since Oct 15). Saves and sends, which each carry reach of their own and travel with profile views and new followers, and clickthroughs to owned distribution. Practical posts are judged on saves; stories on comments where readers describe their own version of the problem. LinkedIn is the discovery layer, the list is the business. This is list-feeding, not audience-building. Each quarter, check who you reached against who buys: owners and founders of small businesses, not your old real estate network.

07

Standing guardrails

→

LinkedIn is discovery, never the destination. If it throttles you tomorrow, you shrug — the relationship was never banked there.

→

The profile is part of every post. Headline says what you build. It files you into the right room before a word of the post is read.

→

Reject the vulnerability loop. No performative emotional striptease. Expertise travels now — you don't have to bleed for reach.

→

Sustainability is a strategy input. A cadence you'll ghost in two weeks is worse than a lighter one you'll keep. Design for the you that gets bored.

→

You write every post. From Nov 28 every post is yours from the first word. Claude helps before you write (angles, questions to answer, hook ideas when you ask) and after (line edits, the review, the log). It never writes a draft or a proposed version. Copied AI posts lose 25 to 35% of their reach; AI-assisted posts with your own detail lose nothing. Outlines on request when a slot is stuck.

→

Charleston stays off this feed. CHS, TFG, and the local layer are a different audience and a different job. This surface is New NEXT only.

→

Original and on-lane, or don't post. The algorithm rewards what AI can't fake: your real screenshots, your own takes. And on-topic 8 of 10 beats 6 of 10 by 30%, so drift isn't neutral, it taxes you. The firewall above is now a ranking advantage, not just discipline.

→

Swear like you talk, not for effect. Your call, Nov 28: it's in, sparingly, with a point, never aimed at a person. The report's one warning stands: a spelled-out f-word can get a post flagged or held back, and masked versions (f#ck) aren't flagged today. Mask by default on this feed; spell it out only when it's worth the risk.

→

Em dashes are back. Your call, Nov 28. Posts with one reach 1.02x the same creator's posts without, so they aren't the AI tell the old rule assumed. Use your natural unspaced dash where it reads well; keep it to one or two a post, because density was the tell. "It's not X. It's Y." stays banned.

→

No complaining about LinkedIn, no generated pictures. Posts that criticise the platform lose 15 to 35% of their reach. AI-generated images lose 22% of reach and up to 40% of engagement; real photos and screenshots win, and a made graphic must carry a number, chart or diagram.

→

Sell through stories, never launch posts. Launch and webinar posts reach about half a normal post. When there's something to sell or start, tell the story that made it, with the link in the body. Added Nov 28, 2026.
