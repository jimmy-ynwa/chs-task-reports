Chapter 5

The pitch: say you know they advertise elsewhere, then show three things only you do

Never "competitor." The email treats their CHStoday spend as a smart move, then wins on how many ads share the page, who writes the ad, the Charleston-only reader data, and the results page you send after every run. It never argues reach or clicks.

To: first name at business · From: Jimmy · Sent as: a new email, not a reply
Subject, pick one:
1. You're already in Charleston inboxes. One more idea
2. Business + CHS Happenings
3. Saw your specific ad. Smart move

Hi first name,

OPENER A · Name it, give credit

I saw specific ad in CHStoday this month. Smart move. Charleston inboxes are where people actually make weekend plans, and you're clearly already sold on email.

OPENER B · Don't name it

I noticed business has been showing up in another Charleston newsletter this fall, specific ad included. So you already know email works for you. I'm not here to talk you out of that.

OPENER C · The second room

You're already reaching Charlestonians by email. I caught specific ad. I'm writing because there's a second room full of your customers you're not in yet.

I'm Jimmy Farrell. I write CHS Happenings, a Charleston newsletter that lands Monday and Thursday mornings with 11,800+ subscribers, and about 8,400 of them open every issue. Here's how it works differently:

  • Your ad has the room to itself. Each issue carries up to three sponsors, never a wall of ads. You're not competing with a dozen other offers for the same glance.
  • I write your ad myself. Same voice as the rest of the issue, built around one offer and a real reason to act now, so it reads like part of the newsletter and not like an interruption.
  • It keeps working after the email. Every story lives on its own page at chshappenings.com, and your ad rides along at the top. Those pages get read all week from Google, Nextdoor and shares.
  • You know exactly who's reading. My reader numbers come from my own Charleston subscribers, with anyone who's unsubscribed taken out: 48% have household incomes over $150K, 73% own their homes, and 45% hold a graduate degree.
  • You get a receipt. After every run I send a results page: clicks from the email, clicks from the web pages, and what I'd change in the next ad.

Don't take my word for it. Here are the numbers, and link to a sample results page shows exactly what you'd get back after a run.

Why-now line Worth 15 minutes this week to see if a short run fits alongside what you're already doing?

Jimmy

Why it's built this way

“Alongside what you're already doing” keeps it additive. They never have to admit CHStoday was a mistake, and you never call it a competitor.

Claims I left out on purpose

  • Reach, open rate or clicks versus CHStoday. They win or tie on all three.
  • Anything about the quality of their other ads. On a daily network newsletter the sponsor shares the page with national affiliate offers (THC gummies, insurance quotes, adult toys ran this month). Opener B and the “room to itself” bullet imply it without saying it.

Confirm before it goes out

  • “Up to three sponsors.” Primary, Secondary, and Featured Event or Short Take. Does the Noticeboard count? If it reads as more ads to a sponsor, say “three sponsor spots, plus a reader noticeboard.”
  • Sample results page. You'll want one you can share. Fleet Feet or Dermhaus with their OK, or a version with the name removed.
  • No network ads. Beehiiv shows $0 in ad-network earnings over the last four weeks, so “no national ad-network offers” is accurate today if you want to say it outright.

Your standing rules applied

  • Second person to a named contact, even at a team inbox
  • Signed “Jimmy,” first person, no “we”
  • Points to the web posts as a selling point
  • Closes on a run, not a one-off ad
  • Live numbers, floor written as 11,800+
